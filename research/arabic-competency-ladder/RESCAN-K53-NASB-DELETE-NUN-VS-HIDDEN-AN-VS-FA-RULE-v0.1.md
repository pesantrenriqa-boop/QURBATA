# Rescan K53 — Nasb `حذف النون` vs Hidden `أن` vs `فاء جواب الشرط` Rule v0.1

**Status:** WORKING RESEARCH — PRE-FREEZE  
**Baseline:** K1–K52 DRAFT-FROZEN.

## Purpose

Menentukan operasi baru paling ringan setelah K52 tanpa membuat K baru hanya karena sebuah pola lama dipindahkan ke governor baru.

---

## Candidate A — Nasb via `حذف النون`

### Available foundation
- K51 already teaches recognition of inflectional nūn deletion in a familiar mudhari‘ pattern;
- K52 teaches explicit nāṣib → mudhari‘ → nasb relation.

### Learner operation
Recognize that the same familiar nūn-bearing mudhari‘ pattern loses its nūn under a validated nasb environment.

### Distinctness test
The deletion mechanism itself is already mastered at K51. What changes is the governing environment from jazm to nasb.

This is pedagogically valuable **transfer**, but not necessarily a new atomic operation.

**Judgement: HIGH application value, LOW K distinctness. Prefer transfer layer unless evidence shows confusion requiring independent assessment.**

---

## Candidate B — Hidden/estimated `أن` (`أن مضمرة`) in a tightly controlled nasb environment

### New operation
Learner infers a non-overt governor that licenses nasb of a following mudhari‘ in a validated local construction.

### Dependency burden
Moderate–high:
- K52 provides visible nasb effect and explicit-governor logic;
- hidden `أن` adds one reconstruction step at the governor level;
- examples must avoid disputed governance and broad traditional taxonomies.

### Scope possibility
Only occurrences where:
- the surface construction that licenses estimation is already familiar;
- one hidden governor is sufficient;
- mudhari‘ form is transparent;
- no second omitted element is needed;
- traditional analysis is stable enough for teaching.

**Judgement: DISTINCT and a natural progression from overt governor → reconstructed governor.**

---

## Candidate C — Rule-level generalization of when `جواب الشرط` takes `فـ`

### New operation
Generalize from occurrence recognition (K48) to a rule predicting/justifying fā’ with multiple response categories.

### Dependency burden
High:
- nominal sentence responses;
- الطلبية;
- جامدة;
- particles such as `قد`, `لن`, `ما`, `سوف`, etc.;
- multiple response-type categories.

This is not one simple operation unless heavily restricted.

**Judgement: important but still too taxonomy-heavy for K53 core.**

---

## Candidate D — broader explicit nāṣib inventory

Adding more overt particles does not automatically add a new operation. If the learner already maps overt nāṣib → nasb effect, more governors are mastery breadth unless a particle introduces a genuinely different dependency.

**Judgement: application/mastery, not K by default.**

---

## Head-to-head decision

**K53-CAND — infer one hidden `أن` in a tightly controlled, occurrence-specific nasb construction.**

Reason:
1. direct progression from K52 overt governor to one omitted governor;
2. adds exactly one reconstruction operation;
3. more distinct than simply transferring K51 deletion to nasb;
4. less taxonomy-heavy than full `فاء جواب الشرط` rule generalization.

## Provisional K53 definition

Learner can recognize, in a validated simple Qur'anic occurrence, that a following mudhari‘ is mansūb through **one locally licensed but non-overt `أن`**, without learning the full traditional inventory of places where `أن` is obligatorily or permissibly omitted.

## Hard exclusions

K53 does not teach:
- full `أن المضمرة` taxonomy;
- disputed governor analysis;
- multiple omitted governors/elements;
- nasb forms whose morphology is itself not already understood;
- broad semantic theory of purpose/result until separately warranted;
- advanced coordination/subordination reconstruction.

## Evidence classes for next gate

- **PASS-A:** one hidden `أن`; local licensing construction familiar; transparent mudhari‘ nasb; all else ≤ K52.
- **PASS-B:** cumulative-clean extra material only.
- **REVIEW-LICENSE:** hidden `أن` analysis is standard but explaining why it is licensed would require a new rule family.
- **REVIEW-ANALYSIS:** more than one plausible grammatical account.
- **PREMATURE:** multiple ellipses, advanced coordination, disputed analysis, or morphology beyond K52.
