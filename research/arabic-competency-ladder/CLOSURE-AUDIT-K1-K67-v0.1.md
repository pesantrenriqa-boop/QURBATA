# CLOSURE AUDIT — Qur'anic Arabic Competency Ladder K1–K67 v0.1

**Status:** CLOSURE AUDIT — PASS WITH CONSOLIDATION REQUIRED  
**Baseline:** K1–K67 DRAFT-FROZEN  
**Endpoint hypothesis:** K67 is the provisional endpoint of the core ladder.

## 1. Purpose

This audit tests whether the ladder should continue adding numbered competencies or close the core sequence and move remaining material into mastery, transfer, advanced grammar, balaghah, or corpus expansion.

The governing principle remains:

> A new traditional rule, particle, subtype, or terminology does not automatically justify a new K. A new K requires a genuinely distinct learner operation with sufficient clean Qur'anic evidence and manageable prerequisites.

## 2. Closure dimensions

The K1–K67 sequence is audited across six dimensions:

1. **form recognition** — identifying relevant overt forms and markers;
2. **local structure** — identifying constituent/clause roles and boundaries;
3. **dependency** — resolving grammatical or referential links;
4. **clause architecture** — reading relations among propositions;
5. **semantic/discourse relation** — sequence, contrast, result, reason, purpose, restriction, concession;
6. **controlled Qur'anic transfer** — applying earlier operations inside increasingly complex but still analyzable verses.

Finding: the ladder now spans all six dimensions. Remaining frontier topics mostly deepen or combine these operations rather than introduce a clearly new core operation.

## 3. High-level architecture confirmed

### Layer A — foundational recognition and phrase construction
Earlier K units establish the lexical/morphological and local phrase operations needed before clause analysis.

### Layer B — sentence and clause structure
The middle ladder progressively establishes nominal/verbal structures, operators, dependencies, and conditional architecture.

### Layer C — interclausal and reference operations
Later units include coordination, temporal sequencing, relative-clause boundaries, and explicit anaphoric linkage.

### Layer D — discourse-semantic relations
The late ladder now contains a controlled set of distinct relations:
- K58 coordination;
- K59 temporal sequencing;
- K62 contrast/correction;
- K63 result/consequence;
- K64 cause/reason;
- K65 exception/restriction;
- K66 purpose/goal;
- K67 concession/counterexpectation.

This is sufficient to establish a core transition from grammar recognition to Qur'anic discourse comprehension without turning the ladder into a complete catalogue of balaghah.

## 4. Frontier queue audit

### Omitted `العائد`
**Disposition:** ADVANCED/MASTERY, not immediate new K.
Reason: operation depends on null-element reconstruction plus licensing/transitivity knowledge. It is a legitimate advanced topic but currently too dependency-heavy for the core endpoint.

### Full `باب الاستثناء`
**Disposition:** MASTERY under/after K65.
Reason: `تام/ناقص`, `موجب/غير موجب`, and detailed post-`إلا` i‘rab mostly deepen the same exception architecture or add case-rule detail.

### Full relative-pronoun inventory
**Disposition:** FORM MASTERY.
Reason: inventory expansion is not a new learner operation.

### Detailed taxonomy of `ف`
**Disposition:** ADVANCED DISAMBIGUATION / TRANSFER.
Reason: multifunctionality creates classification burden. Resultative occurrences can already instantiate K63 after validation.

### Elaborative/specification relations
**Disposition:** FRONTIER RESEARCH.
Reason: potentially valuable, but no sufficiently stable overt marker family has yet justified a new core K.

### Advanced rhetorical relations
**Disposition:** BALAGHAH LAYER.
Reason: when relation recognition depends primarily on rhetorical inference or tafsir rather than overt linguistic evidence, it should not be forced into this core linguistic ladder.

### Long-distance reference / nested clauses
**Disposition:** ADVANCED TRANSFER.
Reason: combines existing boundary and reference-resolution operations at higher complexity.

## 5. Redundancy audit

Late competencies survive the anti-redundancy test because they ask different questions:

- K58: are A and B coordinated?
- K59: what follows what temporally?
- K60: where is the relative-clause boundary?
- K61: which overt element refers back to the relative antecedent?
- K62: what is corrected/contrasted?
- K63: what is the result of what?
- K64: what is the reason for what?
- K65: what domain is restricted and what is excepted/specified?
- K66: for what intended goal is A done?
- K67: what remains true despite an expectation-triggering circumstance?

No pair above is reducible to the same learner operation if assessment prompts are kept distinct.

## 6. Dependency audit

Core closure requires that later K items remain solvable with earlier operations plus exactly one target operation.

Audit rule:

`Item complexity may increase; operation novelty must remain singular.`

The late gates already enforce this through exclusion of:
- hidden `أن` as a new operation;
- unvalidated multifunctional particles;
- omitted resumptives;
- new post-`إلا` case rules;
- tafsir-only causal/concessive inference;
- nested discourse relations.

Finding: dependency policy is coherent enough for provisional closure.

## 7. Evidence audit

A distinction is necessary between:

### A. competency architecture
The K definition is distinct and pedagogically valid.

### B. corpus bank maturity
The number, distribution, and diversity of Qur'anic examples may still need expansion and validation.

Therefore `DRAFT-FROZEN` does **not** mean corpus work is complete. It means the competency identity is frozen against casual renumbering/redefinition.

Remaining evidence work should expand examples **inside K1–K67**, not automatically generate K68+.

## 8. Endpoint decision

### K68 stop-gate result
No currently scanned frontier candidate satisfies all of:
- distinct new learner operation;
- clean overt Qur'anic evidence;
- manageable prerequisites;
- non-redundancy with K1–K67;
- enough pedagogical value to belong to the core ladder.

### Decision

> **K67 is accepted as the PROVISIONAL CORE ENDPOINT.**

K68–K70 are not reserved, required, or considered missing. The former K70 horizon is retired as a planning heuristic.

A future K68 may be created only after new corpus research demonstrates a genuinely distinct operation that passes the same gates.
