## Target
K45: recognize and reconstruct a deleted resumptive (‘ā’id maḥżūf) in a Qur'anic relative-clause environment only when the deletion is locally recoverable and grammatically licensed.

## Positive occurrence classes

### P1 — deleted nominal/predicative resumptive
QS 43:84: `وَهُوَ الَّذِي فِي السَّمَاءِ إِلَٰهٌ`
Canonical i‘rāb analysis explicitly reconstructs a deleted subject/resumptive inside the ṣilah: `هو الذي هو في السماء إله` and identifies the deleted element as the ‘ā’id.
Use: strongest explicit positive control because the grammatical analysis itself names the deletion and reconstruction.

### P2 — deleted object resumptive with transitive verb
Qur'anic relative structures in which the relative antecedent fills an object slot may license deletion of the attached object resumptive when the governing verb is complete/transitive and the antecedent uniquely supplies the missing argument.
Assessment rule: only retain an occurrence when the missing object position is uniquely recoverable from local syntax; do not infer deletion merely because a relative pronoun precedes a verb.

### P3 — deleted governed/prepositional resumptive
Relative-clause constructions may omit a resumptive governed by a preposition only under licensed structural conditions. These are advanced examples and must be excluded from automatic scoring unless the governing preposition and reconstruction are unambiguous.

## Negative controls
N1. Relative pronoun + clause with an overt resumptive pronoun: not K45-positive.
N2. Relative pronoun functioning as an argument without any deleted internal resumptive: not K45-positive.
N3. Indefinite noun followed by a descriptive clause without an explicit relative pronoun: do not misclassify as deleted resumptive.
N4. Cases requiring tafsir-level ellipsis rather than local grammatical reconstruction: exclude from K45 assessment bank.
