## Positive controls
### P1 — Qur'an 43:84
Canonical i'rab reconstructs the relative clause as: `هو الذي هو في السماء إله`; the omitted `هو` is explicitly identified as the resumptive (`العائد`). This remains the strongest anchor occurrence.

### P2 — Qur'an 9:1
In `إلى الذين عاهدتم من المشركين`, the grammatical analysis treats `من المشركين` as a circumstantial phrase related to an omitted resumptive in the relative clause, reconstructable through the object relation of `عاهدتموهم`. This supplies a different surface realization from P1.

### P3 — Qur'an 4:25
The analysis of `فمن ما ملكت أيمانكم من فتياتكم` identifies `من فتياتكم` as related to an estimated resumptive (`العائد المقدر`), with the reconstruction corresponding to `ملكته`. This provides another independent lexical/syntactic host.

## Boundary / negative controls
### N1 — Qur'an 10:59
The verse contains more than one omitted element, including an omitted resumptive associated with `ما أنزله الله` and another omitted argument inferred from context. It is retained as a boundary example, not a clean auto-scored item, because multiple ellipses increase ambiguity.

### N2 — Qur'an 2:90
The grammar contains a visible resumptive pronoun in `يشاؤه` and other omitted elements elsewhere. This is useful to ensure the learner does not classify every relative-clause pronoun or every omission as K45.

### N3 — Qur'an 7:43
The analysis refers to a resumptive inside an estimated relative-clause structure. Because the relative structure itself is partly reconstructed, this is excluded from the primary clean bank and retained as a hard negative/boundary case.
