# RESCAN K60 — `ف` Sequencing vs Relative Structure vs Causal Relation v0.1

**Status:** CANDIDATE SELECTION  
**Baseline:** K1–K59 DRAFT-FROZEN.

## Purpose

K60 must add a genuinely new learner operation. It must not simply enumerate another conjunction after K58–K59 or duplicate earlier clause analysis.

## Candidate A — immediate/close sequencing with `ف`

Potential operation:

`clause 1 + ف + clause 2 → recognize ordered progression with close succession`

### Strengths
- natural extension after K58 coordination and K59 `ثم` sequencing;
- enables contrast between neutral coordination and two kinds of sequencing;
- overt surface marker.

### Risks
- Qur'anic `ف` is highly multifunctional;
- may be `فاء جواب الشرط`, causal/resultative, explanatory, connective, or other functions;
- learner may succeed by memorizing particle labels rather than analyzing relation;
- distinguishing temporal immediacy from result/consequence can require discourse semantics.

### Verdict
**DEFER as K60 core.**
`ف` should first be function-filtered. A clean subset may later support transfer or a dedicated relation competence.

---

## Candidate B — simple relative-clause attachment

Potential operation:

`اسم موصول ظاهر + صلة موصول مكتملة → identify the clause that completes/restricts the relative expression`

Learner identifies an overt relative marker and recognizes that the following clause functions as its `صلة الموصول`.

### Strengths
- structurally distinct from coordination and condition;
- marker is overt in core cases;
- relation is asymmetric and local;
- high Qur'anic utility;
- learner operation is boundary + attachment recognition, not inventory memorization;
- can begin with familiar overt forms such as `الذي` / `الذين` without requiring the complete relative-pronoun paradigm.

### Risks
- resumptive pronoun (`العائد`) can introduce extra complexity;
- some relative clauses have long spans;
- complete theory of الموصولات would overload the competency.

### Containment
Core K60 can require only:
1. overt familiar `اسم موصول`;
2. a short, independently analyzable following clause;
3. recognition that the clause is attached as `صلة الموصول`;
4. no requirement yet to analyze every `عائد` configuration.

### Verdict
**STRONGEST CANDIDATE.**

---

## Candidate C — causal/result relation between clauses

Potential operation:

`event/proposition A → overt connector → result/consequence B`

### Strengths
- semantically useful;
- common discourse relation;
- could build beyond pure syntax.

### Risks
- many surface markers are multifunctional;
- causality may be inferred semantically rather than grammatically marked;
- could overlap with `ف`, conditional response, purpose constructions, and lexical semantics.

### Verdict
**DEFER.** Requires a cleaner marker-specific evidence gate before competency numbering.

### Verdict
**MASTERY/TRANSFER unless a new representation operation appears.** Do not return to textbook enumeration merely to fill K numbers.

# Selection

## K60-CAND

> **Mengenali `صلة الموصول` sebagai klausa yang melekat pada `اسم موصول` eksplisit dalam konstruksi Qurani sederhana dan tervalidasi.**

Core representation:

`[اسم موصول] + [جملة صلة الموصول]`

## New learner operation

The learner must:
1. detect the overt relative marker;
2. find the following clause boundary;
3. attach that clause to the relative marker as its complementing relative clause.

This is not peer coordination (K58), temporal sequencing (K59), or condition→response linkage.

## Initial scope

Prefer:
- overt familiar relative markers;
- short verbal or nominal relative clauses analyzable ≤ K59;
- locally clear boundary;
- no advanced `العائد` analysis required for first-stage items.

Reject:
- long nested relative clauses;
- omitted/complex resumptives;
- ambiguous attachment;
- full `الأسماء الموصولة` inventory;
- simultaneous new morphology.

## Decision

**Advance Candidate B to K60 evidence/distinctness gate.**

`ف` sequencing and causal relations remain in the frontier queue and must not be forced into K60 simply because they are traditional next topics.
